# Ball bounce against a surface

<!-- BEGIN PLAIN ENGLISH -->
## Plain-English explanation

Make a ball bounce off a wall or floor.

1. Describe which way the surface faces using a short arrow called a normal.
2. If the ball is already moving away, do not bounce it again.
3. Reverse the part of its speed going into the surface.
4. Use restitution, a number from zero to one, to choose how much bounce remains. Sideways speed stays unchanged.

See [PORTS.md](PORTS.md) for actual inputs, outputs and a worked example. The [beginner guide](../../docs/beginner-guide.md) explains common coding words.
<!-- END PLAIN ENGLISH -->

## Mechanic and algorithm

Reflect incoming velocity along a surface normal and scale its normal component with a restitution coefficient. Normals may be any nonzero length. Outgoing velocity is unchanged.

## Assumptions and limits

Infinite stationary frictionless surface, no contact detection, spin, rolling, drag, moving surfaces, or collision between finite-mass bodies.

Tune the rules for your game and extend the tests before integrating. Educational coverage is not a full genre implementation or a production-readiness guarantee.
